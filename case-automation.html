Background

What is Automation?

Automation allows businesses to streamline repetitive tasks by setting up predefined rules — a simple yet powerful logic: “When this happens, do that, but only if certain conditions are met.”

For example, automation can send follow-up emails after an estimate is created, assign jobs to available technicians, or mark invoices as overdue after a set time. This helps businesses save time, reduce human error, and maintain workflow consistency.

Problem

Why was this needed?

Many workflows required greater flexibility in conditions and triggers. Additionally, a usability issue in the previous version caused user confusion during the flow. This presented a great opportunity to improve the user experience while also expanding functionality.

One of the key challenges was the risk of disruption — since businesses heavily relied on the existing system, any changes needed to be seamless and non-disruptive to maintain user trust and workflow efficiency.

Goals & success metrics

Boost adoption and efficiency

The automation revamp aimed to boost adoption and efficiency. The goal was to streamline users into the jobs funnel, increasing jobs closed by 10% in Q1 and 20% in Q2. Additionally, it sought to increase feature adoption, ensuring 50% of accounts used automation to enhance engagement and workflow efficiency.

Design process

Preserve familiarity. Introduce a modern, scalable experience

The core design principle was preserving familiarity for existing users while introducing a modern, intuitive, and scalable experience. The system needed to be clear, updated, and approachable — avoiding complexity that could discourage automation adoption.

  • Exploration & validation — created multiple variations of the automation model and its building blocks while collaborating closely with Product and Development teams to ensure feasibility.
  • Wireframing & user testing — built high-fidelity wireframes to test workflow clarity and usability early, identifying pain points through user feedback and testing sessions.
  • Building a scalable & familiar solution — developed rule states, title input states, and select menus to maintain a unified look and feel within the Workiz design system, simplifying updates.
  • Feedback & iteration — refined designs from low-fidelity to high-fidelity, incorporating stakeholder and user feedback so new users could adopt the system quickly while existing users stayed comfortable.
